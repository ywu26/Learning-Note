\section*{Learning method}
Build physical meaning before manipulating formulas. For each concept, identify what can be observed or measured, state the mathematical definition, and distinguish it from a physical law or theorem. Stay within the requested concept and advance only after the learner confirms understanding. A planned subject is not a completed lesson.

\section*{Proposed sequence}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Book-led foundations through Maxwell's equations.} Continue Chapter 1, then study potential and conductors in Chapters 2--3. Work selected problems to check derivations. Follow the book toward magnetism, induction, Maxwell's equations, and waves, explaining experimental assumptions and mathematical steps before advancing.
\item \textbf{Displacement current and charge conservation.} Examine a charging capacitor, compare surfaces sharing a boundary, and explain why the time-dependent electric-field term is needed.
\item \textbf{Fields at conductors and energy flow.} Introduce surface charge, surface current, ideal-conductor boundary conditions, the Poynting vector, and field energy with explicit assumptions.
\item \textbf{Transmission lines as guided fields.} Connect a TEM line's transverse electric field to voltage, magnetic circulation to current, and field storage to distributed capacitance and inductance.
\item \textbf{Reflection at an ideal short and open.} Fix current and voltage reference directions. Explain how the conductor responds, how fields superpose, and how backward energy propagation can coexist with a reflected current of the same sign. Distinguish incident, reflected, and total quantities.
\item \textbf{Apply the picture to the AN720 antenna.} First identify the exact model, feed, conductor connections, substrate, and relevant geometry using verified documentation and photographs. Then connect current/charge distributions, resonance, matching, polarization, and radiation to the confirmed structure.
\end{enumerate}

\section*{Suggested books and reading path}
These are recommendations based on the learner's preference for experimental and physical meaning before algebra. Coverage is checked against publisher information; suitability and sequence are editorial judgments.

\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Current primary text: Purcell and Morin, Electricity and Magnetism, 3rd edition (2013).} Reading began with Chapter 1: charges, fields, flux, and Gauss. Chapters 2--3 develop potential and conductors; Chapters 5--6 cover moving charges and magnetism; Chapter 7 covers induction; Chapter 9 covers Maxwell and waves. Chapters 10--11 address fields in matter and magnetic moments. This text supplies the physical foundations for later transmission-line and antenna study.\\
\href{https://www.cambridge.org/za/titles/electricity-and-magnetism-3rd-edition}{Cambridge publisher page}.
\item \textbf{For systematic mathematical derivations: Griffiths, Introduction to Electrodynamics, 5th edition.} Chapter 1 covers vector analysis; Chapters 2--7 develop electrostatics through electrodynamics; later chapters cover conservation, waves, fields, and radiation. Use as a rigorous companion when ready for more calculation. It is a physics foundation rather than an RF hardware design text.\\
\href{https://www.cambridge.org/highereducation/books/introduction-to-electrodynamics/FD23E188E2BDCDB40199CFE3386EC08F}{Cambridge publisher page}.
\item \textbf{Then connect fields to wireless hardware: Pozar, Microwave Engineering, 4th edition.} Prioritize Chapters 1--3 (fields, transmission lines, waveguides), Chapter 4 (network analysis), and Chapter 5 (matching). It directly supports the planned impedance/reflection and RF work, but its initial EM review is not a substitute for the foundational explanations requested here.\\
\href{https://bcs.wiley.com/he-bcs/Books?action=contents&bcsId=6874&itemId=0470631554}{Wiley companion contents}.
\item \textbf{For antenna analysis and design: Balanis, Antenna Theory: Analysis and Design, 4th edition.} Use after field propagation and transmission-line basics. Its radiation, antenna parameters, impedance, polarization, and antenna-geometry treatment is relevant to later AN720 analysis. Identify the antenna's actual structure before selecting a model.\\
\href{https://bcs.wiley.com/he-bcs/Books?action=contents&bcsId=9777&itemId=1118642066}{Wiley antenna-text companion site}.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Free supplementary reading:} The Feynman Lectures, Volume II, especially Chapters 2--3 for vector fields and Chapters 16--18 for induction and Maxwell. They are useful for physical explanations, alongside a structured textbook and exercises.\\
\url{https://www.feynmanlectures.caltech.edu/II_toc.html}

A practical sequence is Purcell--Morin for understanding, Pozar for guided fields and RF, then Balanis for antennas. Griffiths can supply more detailed mathematical practice in parallel. No single text replaces careful distinction between a definition, a mathematical identity, and an experimentally supported law.
